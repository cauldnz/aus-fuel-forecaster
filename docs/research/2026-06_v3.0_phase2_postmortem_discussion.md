# v3.0 Phase 2 postmortem — why is the augmentor surface flat?

**Date:** 2026-06-03
**Status:** discussion / research artefact — no decision yet
**Reads after:** [`2026-06_v3.0_phase2_outcome.md`](2026-06_v3.0_phase2_outcome.md)
**Context:** 8 of 8 v2.x augmentor variants showed "noise" or "weak" verdict under v3.0 k-fold. This doc unpacks **why** that might be, with literature support, and proposes concrete next-step experiments for each of three competing readings.

The user explicitly asked for deeper treatment of Reading C ("different model / different feature engineering") so that section is longest, but Readings A and B get honest treatment because the disambiguation between them matters for which experiment to run next.

## Part 1 — what the variance structure tells us

Before debating "why", let's look at what the data actually says about how noisy it is. Per-fold metrics for the PR B baseline:

| Fold | Test window | MAE A | MAE B | Δ MAE |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 2020-05 → 2021-04 | 6.219 | 6.293 | +0.074 |
| 2 | 2021-05 → 2022-04 | 8.799 | 8.664 | −0.135 |
| 3 | 2022-05 → 2023-04 | 13.331 | 13.591 | +0.260 |
| 4 | 2023-05 → 2024-04 | 6.954 | 6.855 | −0.098 |
| 5 | 2024-05 → 2025-04 | 4.181 | 4.327 | +0.147 |
| 6 | 2025-05 → 2026-04 | 9.573 | 10.615 | **+1.042** |
| Mean | — | 8.176 | 8.391 | **+0.215** |
| Stdev | — | 2.893 | 3.037 | **0.394** |

### Three observations

**1. Per-fold base MAE varies 3.2×** (4.18 c/L on fold 5 to 13.33 c/L on fold 3). This is fold *difficulty*, not estimator noise — 2022's Russia-Ukraine spike is genuinely harder to forecast than the comparatively calm 2024-25 period. Some periods just have less predictable prices.

**2. Δ MAE is a much more precise measurement than per-fold MAE.** Stdev of Δ is 0.394 c/L vs base MAE stdev 2.89 c/L — the paired comparison **divides out** the fold-difficulty signal. This is the whole point of paired comparison: when both models see the same fold, the fold-difficulty term cancels.

**3. Direction is consistent, magnitude is small, fold 6 is an outlier.** Excluding fold 6, the other 5 folds' Δ MAE all sit in [−0.135, +0.260] — i.e. ±0.26 c/L of zero. Fold 6 alone contributes 5× the typical fold's magnitude. **Most of the "Model B loses by 0.215 c/L on average" comes from fold 6.**

### What this means for the design doc's noise rule

The design doc §2.5 rule (`|Mean| > Stdev → robust`) was set conservatively. But the rule treats per-fold variance as if it were sampling noise. Looking at the per-fold Δ values directly:

- 4 of 6 folds: B is worse than A (sign agrees with mean)
- 5 of 6 folds: |Δ MAE| ≤ 0.260 (tight cluster)
- 1 of 6 folds (fold 6): |Δ MAE| = 1.042 (outlier driving the mean + stdev)

The cluster-plus-outlier shape says **the augmentor adds essentially zero in 5 of 6 typical periods, then hurts badly in one spike period.** That's directionally consistent with a real-but-tiny-and-fragile effect, not pure measurement noise.

So a more honest summary than "noise" might be: **"on average the augmentor adds zero ± 0.26 c/L in typical periods; in shock periods it can hurt by ~1 c/L."** That reframing changes what each reading below means.

## Part 2 — Reading A: the augmentor surface is genuinely flat

### The claim

Tree-based models on already-feature-rich tabular forecasting have nothing left to extract from SA2 demographic context, because per-station lag features encode it implicitly. The augmentor adds new columns but no new *information* beyond what `lag_price_28`, `roll_price_mean_28`, `xfuel_dl_price_lag_0`, `stn_competitors_within_5km` etc. already carry.

### Evidence for

- **PR C SHAP analysis** ([`docs/research/2026-05_sa2_feature_curation.md`](2026-05_sa2_feature_curation.md)) found SA2 features all ranked outside the top 20 by gain — top features were lag, brand, calendar, weather. The mean-|SHAP| numbers were single-digit % of headline features.
- **The argument from price-cycle structure**: NSW fuel prices follow a fortnightly cycle that's per-station-deterministic enough that lag_14 + lag_28 should explain 60-80% of variance. Demographics inform the *level* of prices in an area but the model already learns levels from per-station history.
- **GBM literature on tabular forecasting** ([Brenndoerfer 2024](https://mbrenndoerfer.com/writing/boosted-trees-gradient-boosting-complete-guide-algorithm-implementation-scikit-learn)) consistently finds that on rich tabular problems, the marginal value of additional categorical features is small once a few strong continuous features are in the model.
- **Fold 6 outlier interpretation**: even if the augmentor adds tiny value in typical periods, the 2026 spike shows it can actively mislead under regime shift. Demographic features are LEVEL signals, not CHANGE signals — when prices spike from external shocks, "this is an affluent suburb" is the wrong thing to lean on.
- **PR C's curation broadening (E4b alone) made things worse, not better.** Adding the 5 PR-A unmodeled candidates without the density column had Mean Δ MAE +0.377. If demographic context were a real underexploited signal, broader access would help. It didn't.

### Evidence against

- Some folds show B winning marginally (folds 2 + 4, Δ −0.135 and −0.098). Tiny but real.
- We've only tested LightGBM with one hyperparameter set. The "genuinely flat" claim assumes the model class is capacity-appropriate.
- The augmentor's strongest theoretical use case — Centrelink-day × SEIFA interaction — was never given an explicit interaction feature. Trees should learn it implicitly but may need more data / different depth.

### Concrete tests

1. **SHAP on the v3.0 k-fold models per fold** — does the magnitude of SA2 SHAP values vary by fold? If folds where B helps have larger SA2 SHAP and folds where B hurts have smaller, that's evidence the effect is real but unstable. If SA2 SHAP is uniformly tiny, Reading A wins.
2. **Permutation importance per fold** — agree/disagree with SHAP. Robust signal would show up in both.
3. **Train Model A 6× with different random seeds, re-evaluate** — establish a per-fold baseline-vs-baseline Δ MAE distribution. If the baseline-vs-baseline stdev is comparable to baseline-vs-augmented, the entire "noise" is just LightGBM stochasticity. This is the **single most informative experiment** for Reading A and is cheap (~5 hours on the existing harness).

## Part 3 — Reading B: v3.0 methodology is too strict

### The claim

The design doc's `|Mean| > Stdev` rule is too conservative. Naive paired t-test on k-fold scores has dependent samples — every observation appears in (k−1) training folds. [Dietterich 1998](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~jerryzhu/cs769/ttest.pdf) showed this inflates Type I error. The right test is the **Nadeau-Bengio (2003) corrected paired t-test** ([original paper at ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227049463_Inference_for_the_Generalization_Error)), which adjusts the variance estimate to account for fold overlap.

### What Nadeau-Bengio actually does

Per the [correctR vignette](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/correctR/vignettes/correctR.html) and [MachineLearningMastery's guide](https://machinelearningmastery.com/statistical-significance-tests-for-comparing-machine-learning-algorithms/):

The standard paired t-test computes `t = mean(d) / (stdev(d) / sqrt(k))` where `d` is the per-fold delta vector. The Nadeau-Bengio correction multiplies the variance estimate by `(1/k + n_test/n_train)` to account for the fact that the per-fold scores are correlated through shared training data. This **inflates** the variance estimate, making the test **more conservative**, not less.

So Reading B as I originally framed it (~"the test is too strict, real wins are being missed") is **backwards** — Nadeau-Bengio is even stricter than naive paired t-test for k-fold, because it acknowledges the dependence. If we applied it to our results, fewer experiments would clear the bar, not more.

The corrected reading: even our intentionally-conservative |Mean| > Stdev heuristic is **less strict** than what the literature recommends for proper significance testing on k-fold. The v3.0 verdict ("nothing survives") would only get stronger under Nadeau-Bengio.

### What might be too strict

What MIGHT be over-applied is **the way we treat per-fold variance as undirected noise** rather than as informative variation by fold characteristics. From [Liu 2024 in BJMSP](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/bmsp.12330): time-series CV variance has two components — true generalisation noise + systematic period-difficulty differences. We're currently lumping both into "Stdev Δ MAE."

### Concrete tests

4. **Compute Nadeau-Bengio corrected p-values** for the 8 experiments — for completeness, confirm the noise verdict gets stronger not weaker. ~30 min coding effort. If nothing clears p < 0.10 even before correction, Reading B is closed.
5. **Per-period regression**: regress per-fold Δ MAE against fold characteristics (e.g. fold's price volatility, fold's MAE A as a proxy for "how hard is this period"). If Δ MAE varies systematically with fold difficulty, the variance isn't pure noise. Possibly the augmentor helps in stable periods and hurts in volatile periods — that's an actionable finding, not "noise".
6. **Fold-rank consistency**: across the 8 experiments, do the same folds always show B winning vs losing? If fold 2 always favours B and fold 6 always punishes B, the per-experiment differences are systematic; if rankings shuffle, the per-experiment differences are noise. Quick analysis — already have the data.

## Part 4 — Reading C: wrong model / wrong features (the deep dive)

### The claim

GBM with our fixed hyperparameters and the current feature presentation may be the wrong tool to extract the augmentor's value. Three sub-hypotheses:

- **C1 — Capacity mismatch**: `num_leaves=63, n_estimators=2000, early_stopping_rounds=100` may be over- or under-tuned for the augmentor-extended feature set. We use the same hyperparameters for Model A (88 features) and broadened Model B (94 features) — yet the optimal config likely differs.
- **C2 — Interactions not extracted**: GBM finds interactions implicitly but with a fixed tree depth, only depth-N interactions get found. The Centrelink-day × SEIFA hypothesis from [`spec.md §13 #3`](../../spec.md) needs a depth-3+ tree to find naturally (day-of-week × day-of-fortnight × SEIFA). With `num_leaves=63` (~depth 6 in a balanced tree), it CAN be found in principle but might not be prioritised by gain.
- **C3 — Wrong model class**: a model that handles non-linear interactions explicitly (FT-Transformer, TabNet, SAINT, even a simple feedforward NN with categorical embeddings) might extract augmentor value that GBM can't.

### Evidence on capacity (C1)

[LightGBM feature engineering guides](https://www.numberanalytics.com/blog/lightgbm-feature-engineering) emphasise that `num_leaves`, `min_data_in_leaf`, and `feature_fraction` interact heavily with feature-set size. Adding columns without retuning can over-regularise (the model treats the extra features as noise via `feature_fraction=0.8` sampling) or under-regularise (deeper splits on irrelevant categoricals).

Our `feature_fraction=0.8` means each tree only sees 80% of features. For a 73-col Model A that's ~58 features per tree; for a 94-col Model B that's ~75 features per tree. The augmentor-extra columns get sampled less often per tree — but the model fits MORE trees (early-stopping kicks in later because we added capacity). The net effect is non-trivial to predict.

**Test**: hyperparameter search for Model B specifically (Optuna or grid). If a different config moves Mean Δ MAE from +0.215 to clearly negative, capacity was the issue.

### Evidence on interactions (C2)

The Centrelink-day × SEIFA hypothesis is the cleanest case. Spec §13 #3 originally hypothesised that fortnightly Centrelink payment cycles affect fuel purchases differently across SEIFA quintiles (poorer suburbs have stronger fortnightly cycles). [`docs/research/2026-05_centrelink_seifa_interaction.md`](2026-05_centrelink_seifa_interaction.md) tested this directly and reported a "null result" — but only via SHAP-interaction-values inspection, not via an explicit feature.

LightGBM's interaction detection ([microsoft/LightGBM#2884](https://github.com/microsoft/LightGBM/issues/2884)) finds interactions by greedy split selection. If neither parent (day-of-fortnight, SEIFA-quintile) is individually high-gain, the interaction never gets searched. SEIFA's individual SHAP rank was ~46 in PR C; day-of-fortnight ranks higher but the joint search would require depth-N+ where N reaches both. With our `num_leaves=63` setting that's possible but not prioritised.

**Test**: add explicit interaction features (`sa2_seifa_irsd_score * cal_day_of_fortnight`, products of SEIFA with each day-of-week indicator) and re-evaluate under k-fold. If the explicit interaction beats the implicit one, GBM's interaction discovery is leaving signal on the table.

### Evidence on model class (C3)

The recent literature on tabular deep learning is mixed:

- [Brenndoerfer's 2024 GBM guide](https://mbrenndoerfer.com/writing/boosted-trees-gradient-boosting-complete-guide-algorithm-implementation-scikit-learn) and [GeeksforGeeks LightGBM coverage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/machine-learning/lightgbm-light-gradient-boosting-machine/) note that for medium-sized tabular problems (1-10M rows), GBM typically wins or ties NN approaches.
- **However** — for problems where the signal is in feature interactions specifically, NN methods with categorical embeddings can outperform. TabNet, FT-Transformer, NODE, SAINT have all shown ~1-3% improvements on benchmark tabular tasks where interactions dominate.
- Our problem is 15M rows, ~88 features — squarely in the GBM sweet spot. C3 is the least likely of the three sub-hypotheses but worth flagging.

### What would the Reading-C investigation look like?

A focused, time-boxed sequence — each step gates the next:

1. **C1 first (cheap)**: 1-2 nights of Optuna hyperparameter search for Model B against a single fold (say fold 4). If the best config still has Δ MAE ≈ 0, capacity isn't the issue and we move on.
2. **C2 next (medium)**: 1 PR adding explicit Centrelink × SEIFA interaction features, re-evaluated under k-fold. If interactions beat the GBM-implicit baseline, that's a feature-engineering win that survives.
3. **C3 last (expensive)**: only if C1 + C2 both confirm GBM has hit ceiling, then try FT-Transformer or similar. Major framework lift; not worth doing until C1/C2 ruled out.

## Part 5 — what I think is going on

My read, after the variance analysis + research:

**Most likely: Reading A with a Reading-C nuance.** The SA2 augmentor surface is mostly flat because (a) lag-derived features already capture per-station demographic-driven behaviour implicitly, and (b) what residual signal exists is small enough that with our hyperparameters + feature presentation, GBM doesn't reliably find it.

That doesn't mean the augmentor was wasted — the **methodology gain** of v3.0 (knowing this) is the real outcome of the SA2 work. We now know we can stop tweaking demographic features.

**Less likely but worth testing: the Centrelink × SEIFA explicit-interaction test (C2)**. Cheap to run, would tell us if there's specifically an interaction GBM is missing. If yes → engineering opportunity that doesn't require abandoning GBM.

**Closed by the research: Reading B.** Nadeau-Bengio correction makes the test stricter, not looser; the noise verdict wouldn't reverse under it. Variance interpretation could be refined (per-fold direction-consistency, period-difficulty regression) but won't change the headline.

## Part 6 — concrete proposed next steps (one PR each)

Ranked by `signal_value / cost`:

1. **Per-fold rank consistency analysis** [0.5 day, no compute]. Check whether the same folds always show B winning/losing across experiments. Quick disambiguation of Reading A vs C without re-running anything. If folds shuffle randomly, the augmentor signal is genuinely zero; if folds consistently behave the same way across experiments, there's structure to investigate.
2. **Baseline-vs-baseline 6× seed experiment** [5h compute, no new code beyond `train_kfold(... random_state=...)` plumbing]. Run Model A under 6 different LightGBM seeds, all on the same k-fold, and measure baseline-vs-baseline Δ MAE. If the stdev across seeds is comparable to our cross-experiment Δ stdev, the augmentor-experiments are within seed-noise — Reading A confirmed.
3. **Explicit Centrelink × SEIFA interaction feature** [1 day, ~25 min compute]. Add `cal_day_of_fortnight * sa2_seifa_irsd_score` (and a couple of variants) to `feature_blocks.SA2_COLUMNS`, re-evaluate under k-fold. Tests Reading C2 cheaply.
4. **Hyperparameter tuning of Model B against fold 4** [overnight, no new code beyond an Optuna script]. Tests Reading C1 — single-fold tuning is cheap; if best Δ MAE is still ≈ 0, capacity isn't the answer.
5. **Per-period regression on Δ MAE** [0.5 day, no compute beyond pandas]. Quick informative analysis: do periods with higher MAE A (= harder forecasting periods) have systematically different Δ MAE? Or does the augmentor help in some periods and hurt in others independent of difficulty?

I'd suggest **doing #1 + #2 first** as a single discussion-grade outcome, then deciding whether C2 (#3) is worth pursuing based on what #1/#2 reveal. #4 and #5 are stretch goals.
